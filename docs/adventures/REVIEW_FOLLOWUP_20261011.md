Use the existing Quick criteria rather than a new universal reaction-time cutoff, and count each saved answer once.

Carry a held shield through the quest; do not refill hearts or remove retreat. These numbers need a short observed pilot before adoption; the objective is occasional earned recovery while victory remains difficult.

## Proposed word-to-story bridge — not implemented

Author a stable puzzle first, then two or three reviewed language variants with the same answer and visual evidence. Each variant records its target words, other required words, relation being tested, answer, plausible distractors and artwork evidence. Select a suitable variant at the checkpoint using existing word history, then save that exact variant and options; never change it on reload or retry.

Prefer variants whose surrounding vocabulary is familiar and which exercise one or two relevant words. Treat current word history as evidence, not proof of comprehension. A correct choice after the word flash is different from understanding a sentence; log a separate sentence-comprehension observation. Listening/hints remain available and mark that evidence as supported, without changing single-word mastery.

Example using one verified bridge scene:
- Simpler: “The boat is under the bridge. What colour is the boat?”
- More demanding: “Find the boat under the bridge, beside the mill. What colour is it?”
- Distractors must correspond to real other boats so the relation matters. If the actual artwork lacks those objects, this example is invalid and must not ship.

A few preceding battle questions may prioritise already-eligible words needed by the upcoming clue, but due reviews and struggling words keep priority. Cap this authored bias at a small part of the fight (initial proposal: two questions); do not force untaught words into the adaptive pool or repeat a freshly taught word until it looks mastered. If vocabulary is not ready, use the simpler authored variant or normal listening support; never block story progress on a mastery gate.

Revisit a target later in a different scene and with a different relation/answer. Immediate post-teaching success measures supported application, not delayed retention. Include a later unprompted encounter to assess transfer. No live text generation is needed.

Pilot four puzzles in one new subchapter. Audit each variant against the image and each distractor against the clue. Observe independent reading, listening, wrong choices, rereading and next-session transfer; do not use speed on sentence clues. Compare qualitative play experience before expanding throughout the game.

## Fourth major adventure proposal: The Clockwork City Below

After Sky Stars, a newly lit mountain doorway reveals a warm underground city of lifts, crystal windows and tiny workshops. Its great clock has stopped, leaving routes and machines in the wrong positions. Artus and Pip help the residents reconnect the city and discover why the clock stopped. This is a mystery with visible repairs rather than another scattered-item collection. It has no real-time countdown.

Eight proposed subchapters use already-approved, currently unassigned creatures. Encounters are guardian tests or misunderstandings, not claims that helpful residents are villains. Exact artwork, text, task counts and new campaign rules require acceptance before implementation.

Detailed search maps can appear earlier or mid-mission instead of always as a ninth task. New artwork must preserve the approved hero, Pip and creature identities and have manually verified clue evidence.
